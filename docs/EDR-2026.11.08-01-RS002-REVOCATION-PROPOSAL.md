# EDR-2026.11.08-01 — RS-002 Revocation Policies Under Network Partition

**Document state:** PROPOSED FOR REVIEW (prepared 2026-11-07 UTC; 2026-11-08 is the proposed EDR identifier/date, not an assertion of future approval)  
**Target:** RS-002 proposed cooperating-facilities protocol fork  
**Baseline isolation:** RS-001 v1.2 remains FROZEN and unmodified. The RESONA-SI governance baseline remains frozen.  
**Safety status:** Research design only; no demonstrated hardware-in-the-loop guarantee, no authorization for live physical actuation or production deployment.

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a *destination-controlled* physical execution boundary can distinguish requests, valid authorization, command commitment, observed physical effect, and independent evidence when separately operated facilities collaborate and communications fail.

Facility A's remote revocation is not automatically visible at Facility B. A destination separated from an authority cannot infer an unreceived revocation event. Any finite stop guarantee therefore depends on explicitly stated communication, timer, lease, local-interlock, and controller assumptions.

## Governance and scope

- No change or backport to frozen RS-001 v1.2; any RS-002 code, harness, outcomes, and parameter sets must be independently versioned.
- Model/agent confidence, consensus, signed request, HTTP acknowledgment, and queued job do **not** by themselves establish authorization or physical execution.
- Destination-side policy and controller safety interlocks are independent of the requesting agent.
- Record independent agent, transport, destination, and witness evidence streams where feasible; pre-register claim-specific evidence ranking.
- A disagreement or insufficient evidence must remain **INDETERMINATE**, not be promoted by the author, reviewer, or an AI.
- No single actor, including protocol author, operator, model, or witness, may solely certify a successful result.
- Any practical demonstration initially uses a benign, controlled actuator; do not connect to hazardous, clinical, or production machinery without separate safety review.

## Revocation vocabulary and physical boundary

Define distinct measured or bounded quantities:

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `t_remote_rev` | Origin-authority issuance of revocation (remote event; not necessarily observed at destination). |
| `t_dest_visible` | Destination first verifies the revocation or reliably infers authority unavailability under its declared policy. |
| `t_reject` | Destination rejects a subsequently presented command due to revoked/expired/invalid authority. |
| `t_commit` | Atomic destination commitment point after which an actuation may no longer be preventable by software. |
| `t_physical_safe` | Witness-observed entry into the declared physical safe condition, if physically reachable. |
| `L_visibility` | `t_dest_visible - t_remote_rev`, only when clocks/order can be reconciled under declared uncertainty. |
| `L_enforcement` | From destination awareness to enforcement/rejection of new actuation, with a stated measurement definition. |
| `L_cessation` | From relevant intervention to physical safe state; may be unbounded or *not applicable* for irreversible processes. |

Publish an end-to-end revocation horizon only **conditional on** specified assumptions (maximum message delay or local authority freshness, heartbeat validity rules, trusted monotonic timer, controller cycle time, safe-stop capacity). When assumptions fail, explicitly declare `NO FINITE GUARANTEE`; do not claim zero-latency remote revocation.

An acknowledged request, a rejected command, a tripped interlock, and a physically ceased process are separate findings.

## Candidate RS-002 policy comparison — no policy selected

| Policy | Candidate operational rule | Required bounds/assumptions | Primary failure to probe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POL-FC-01 — Fail closed | On verified revocation or expiration/loss of required authority freshness, block **new** actuation; stop only where safe-stop mechanics permit. | Define heartbeat/freshness validity and detector intervals, including dropped revocations while heartbeats continue; `min(message_delay, heartbeat_timeout)` is **not** automatically a worst-case remote-revocation bound. | Harmless jitter causing unnecessary interruption; unsafe interruption of an essential clearing motion; stale authority despite live heartbeats. |
| POL-BC-02 — Bounded capability | Allow only pre-issued, verifiable locally bounded actions while disconnected; reject when time **or** action/energy constraints are exceeded. | Independently enforce a trusted monotonic lease deadline **and** action/stroke/energy counters; declare exposure after remote revocation as both a time interval and maximum action budget. A finite count/energy budget alone is not a finite time horizon. | Stale yet locally permitted action within lease; replay, exhausted counter, timer rollback. |
| POL-FA-03 — Fresh authorization | Require a distinct unconsumed warrant for every consequential actuation, checked against local policy/state at atomic commitment; no implicitly authorized queued work. | Bind warrant to operation, policy version, destination, nonce, freshness and physical commitment boundary. A fresh warrant may still precede remote revocation in flight: **not instantaneous** revocation. | Stale check-to-commit window, out-of-order packets, throughput collapse under latency. |

These are *competing hypotheses*; do not label any one universally safest without environment-specific evidence and hazards analysis.

## Adversarial bench matrix (proposed, not executed)

1. **Communication:** asymmetric packet loss 0–100%; variable delay 10 ms–10 s; dropped revocation with heartbeat continuity; total partition; queued and reordered commands.
2. **Timing:** bounded clock step ±500 ms; separate unbounded slew/compromised time case. Time-based finite guarantees are invalid when trustworthy timing assumptions fail.
3. **Authority:** stale signed policies; revoked capabilities; replayed warrants; contradictory facility policies; authority heartbeat mismatch.
4. **Physical boundary:** benign latch or mock valve cycle with an intentionally irreversible *simulated* commitment; distinguish rejection-before-commit, interlock-prevented actuation, and post-commit action that cannot be stopped.
5. **Evidence:** dropped acknowledgment, duplicate retry, incorrect model claim, disagreement across requester/transport/destination/witness, evidence-reconciliation failure.

## Proposed metrics and acceptance process

- Unauthorized **new** physical actuations after effective local revocation/expiration: count and denominator with confidence interval; no assumed zero.
- Remote revocation visibility latency distribution and explicitly conditional worst-case bound (if any).
- Local enforcement/rejection latency; check-to-commit race frequency.
- Physical safe-state latency or an explicit irreversible/not-applicable classification.
- Authorized throughput, false rejection rate, bounded-capability post-revocation exposure, witness disagreement, missing evidence, and indeterminate outcomes.
- Every trial records operation_id, monotonic event ordering, policy/hash/version, controller state, authority and lease state, fault-injection seed, instrument state, and independent receipts.
- Acceptance thresholds and claim-specific evidence ranking must be preregistered **before** test execution. No empirical results or passed checks are asserted by this EDR.

## Stage boundaries

- **Stage 1:** RS-001 v1.2 single-instrument evidence remains independent and frozen.
- **Stage 2:** RS-002 isolated two-facility synthetic/benign hardware test for revocation under partition.
- **Stage 3:** Future separate versioned heterogeneous multi-facility experiments only after documented Stage 2 assessment.
- Quantum hardware is an optional later endpoint, **not** a prerequisite or an emergent property of classical edge data centers.
